What we teach

Three decisions that shape the next ten years

01

Read the offer

Grants, loans and work-study often sit in one column labeled "aid." We show students how to separate money that is given from money that is owed.

02

Compare on net price

Sticker price says little. Students line up every offer side by side and find the true four-year cost of each school.

03

Borrow with a plan

If loans are part of the answer, students learn the types, the limits, and what a monthly payment looks like against a starting salary.

Our story

It started with two letters on a kitchen table.

In April of her senior year, our founder had two college acceptances and two financial aid letters. Her parents had worked hard to get her to that table, but neither had been to college, and the letters might as well have been written in another language.

One school offered $39,000 in "total aid." The other offered $31,000. The choice looked obvious, so she picked the bigger number.

It was not until sophomore year, when the first loan statement arrived, that she understood what she had signed. More than a third of that "aid" was debt. The school she had turned down would have cost her family less every single year.

Nobody had lied to her. The information was all there on the page. She just had no one to tell her that an award letter is not a bill, that "aid" is not the same as "free," and that the number that matters is the one most letters never print: net price.

She started small. A spreadsheet for a younger cousin. Then a Saturday in a public library meeting room with eleven seniors, a stack of real award letters, and a box of highlighters. By the end of the morning, three of those students had changed their minds about where to enroll.

That morning became Finance Forward. We still bring the highlighters.
